## Key Definitions (§1)

- **Hot Work:** Grinding, welding, thermal or oxygen cutting or heating, and other related heat-producing or spark-producing operations.
- **Welding:** Fusion of two pieces of metal, rendered plastic or liquid by heat or by pressure, or both. Two most common in construction: gas welding and electric arc welding.
- **Hazardous Area:** An area in which flammable liquids, vapours or gases, combustible liquids, dusts or fibres, or other flammable or explosive substances may be present.

## Section 2 — Training and Competency

- OSH training complies with **Element 5** and **Mechanism 7.0**.
- Employers shall ensure employees required to implement this CoP are trained in hot work operations (welding and cutting) and understand the associated risks and control measures.

**Training content — 4 mandatory topics (competency-based):**
1. Systems of work needed for safe hot work operations
2. Types and selection of correct welding and cutting equipment
3. Type, selection and use of PPE
4. Care, maintenance and inspection of welding and cutting equipment

**Retraining trigger:** Whenever a periodic inspection reveals, or there is reason to believe, deviations from or inadequacies in the employee's knowledge of correct hot work operations.

**Training record — 5 mandatory fields:**
1. Name and ID number
2. Emirates ID number
3. Subject(s) of training
4. Date(s) of training
5. Person(s) providing the training

## Section 3.1 — Roles and Responsibilities

### Employer Obligations (7)
1. Hot works plant and equipment (welding/cutting) shall be appropriate for the task and maintained in good working condition
2. All work involving hot works plant and equipment is appropriately planned, organized and supervised
3. Those involved in use of hot works equipment are trained and competent
4. All hot works equipment is inspected on a **daily basis** by a competent person
5. Implement an appropriate **medical surveillance program** per **CoP 5.0**
6. Conduct appropriate **occupational air monitoring** per **Element 7** and take appropriate actions to reduce exposures and monitor health
7. Employees using hot works equipment are provided with appropriate **PPE** per **CoP 2.0** — e.g. shield/helmet with filtered lens; fire resistant gloves; leather apron; boots; leather spats; felt skull-cap or beret; preferably overalls

### Employee Obligations (2)
1. Inspect work equipment before use; report any defect reasonably foreseeable to endanger safety
2. Use safety devices provided with welding and cutting equipment in accordance with training or instruction received

## Section 3.2 — Planning and Assessment

- **No hot works outside a designated area** unless a specific **Hot Work Permit to Work** is in place.
- Hot Work PTW shall comply with **CoP 21.0 — Permit to Work Systems**.
- Risk assessment per **Element 2**.

**Employers shall ensure:**
1. Assessment of various risks; systems of work safe to contractors and the public
2. Effective procedures and control measures in place
3. For **Building and Construction Sector**: hot work requirements in Pre-Tender Safety and Health Plan per **CoP 53.0**
4. For **Building and Construction Sector**: safe systems of work and site rules included in **OSH-CMP** per **CoP 53.0**

**5 hot work specific hazards to assess:**
1. Fire and explosion
2. Burns
3. Fumes, gases and ventilation
4. Electricity
5. Radiation

## Section 3.3 — Hot Work Requirements

### 3.3.1 General Requirements (7)
Employers shall ensure:
1. Material safety data sheets for electrodes, fluxes and coatings made available to employees performing welding
2. Protection of welders and others from burns caused by sparks, hot metal
3. Erection of **screens** to protect persons in the vicinity from harmful radiation rays
4. Use of **welding booths** where reasonably practicable
5. Gas systems (cylinders, regulators, hoses) are in good working order
6. All welding-associated waste placed in a **hot waste crate** as close as reasonably practicable to welding activities (hot waste: slag, shot crucibles, metal off-cuts)
7. **Competent First Aiders** with relevant experience in welding areas, covering: persons overcome by welding fumes/gases; burns; "welding flash" eye injuries

### 3.3.2 Designated Hot Work Area
- A **designated hot work area** is a permanent location designed for hot work — does **not** normally require a PTW.
- Employers shall ensure all hot works undertaken in a designated area where reasonably practicable.
- Access/egress shall be restricted to non-authorised persons.

**Designated hot work area — 5 requirements:**
1. Non-combustible, fire-resistive construction, essentially free of combustibles and flammables
2. Appropriately segregated from adjacent areas
3. Equipped with automatic fire protection or fire extinguishers
4. Inspected and approved by management
5. Appropriate ventilation

### 3.3.3 Fumes and Gases
Employers shall ensure:
1. Appropriate control measures to reduce exposure to harmful fumes and gases, including:
   - Removal of rust inhibitors, paints, degreasers and coatings **prior to welding**
   - Segregation of degreasing operations from welding tasks
   - Positioning of welders away from welding fumes
2. Employees performing welding work monitored for adverse health effects per **CoP 5.0 — Medical Surveillance**
3. Appropriate ventilation in all welding locations: general ventilation; dilution ventilation; or **local exhaust ventilation (LEV)**
4. Note: general or dilution ventilation alone are usually insufficient — LEV inlet point shall be as close as reasonably practicable to actual welding work. Refer: **CoP 52.0 — LEV**

### 3.3.4 Electricity and Radiation (3)
1. All cord-connected electrical welding machines tested per manufacturers' specifications
2. Electrical welding machines connected only to circuits protected by **RCDs** and appropriately earthed
3. Personnel performing welding shall ensure electric cables are not damaged by sparks or hot metal

### 3.3.5 Preventing Fire (8)
1. Move work-piece to a safe location for hot work
2. Remove combustible materials (flammable liquids, wood, paper, textiles, packaging, plastics) from within **10 metres** of the work
3. Ventilate spaces where vapours could accumulate (vehicle pits, trenches)
4. Protect combustible materials that cannot be moved with appropriate guards/covers (metal sheeting, mineral fibre-boards, fire retardant blankets)
5. Check no combustible materials hidden behind walls or partitions (especially for prolonged welding/cutting)
6. Use guards/covers to prevent hot particles passing through floor/wall openings (doorways, windows)
7. Maintain a **continuous fire watch** during work and for at least **one hour afterwards**
8. Keep fire extinguishers nearby

## Section 3.4 — Hazardous Areas

Prior to hot work in hazardous areas, the following precautions shall be taken:
1. **Hot-Work Permit** obtained before any work in a hazardous area where flammable/explosive gases and dusts may be present
2. Responsible person satisfied work may safely proceed issues "Hot-Work Permit" per **CoP 21.0**
3. Identify and control fire hazards (flammable/combustible liquids, gases, vapours, dusts, fibres) within **10 m** of hot work
4. Appropriate ventilation of hot-work area
5. Appropriately locate equipment including emergency firefighting equipment
6. Isolate the area where hot work is performed
7. Provide safe entry and exit from hot-work area
8. **Testing for flammable gas/vapour** in the atmosphere and in any pipe, drum, tank, vessel and equipment adjacent to or involved in hot work
9. Ensure concentration of flammable gas/vapour is less than **5% of its Lower Explosion Limit (LEL)**
10. Where specified by hot-work permit: **firewatcher** stationed in area for safe conduct of work
11. Site/work area secured overnight and at expiry of hot-work permit period
12. At completion: equipment returned to normally secure mode; firefighting equipment returned to normal storage
13. At completion: **Hot-Work Permit signed off**

## Section 3.5 — Electric Arc Welding (7)
1. Arc cutting and welding equipment installed and used per manufacturers' specifications
2. Current for electric arc welding (direct or alternating) — voltage as low as consistent with efficient welding
3. Welding leads and return cables shall: have insulation appropriate for hard usage; be examined **at least daily** for defects; be as flexible as reasonably practicable at electrode holder end; have welding return section not less than welding lead return
4. Joints between cable sections made with insulated cable couplings appropriately shrouded (no live metal exposed when separated)
5. Welding return firmly connected to metal by a well-constructed clamp
6. Electrode holder fully insulated (live portions cannot be touched accidentally)
7. When arc welding suspended for substantial periods (lunch, overnight): power source de-energised; electrodes removed from holders; holders placed to prevent accidental contact or arcing

---

## Section 3.6 — Gas Welding (8)
1. No fittings on oxy-acetylene equipment contaminated with grease or oil under any circumstances
2. Regulators checked **at least daily** — failure modes: regulator creep (forward flow) and reverse flow
3. Never use equipment with a known regulator creep condition
4. Use correct colour and type of hoses and fittings per manufacturers' specifications
5. **Copper never used on acetylene lines** (substances formed may spontaneously detonate)
6. **Flashback arresters fitted to all oxy-acetylene equipment** to prevent flashback danger
7. Oxy-acetylene equipment not left near hot equipment or metals that could burn leads
8. No smoking when welding or near welding activities

## Section 3.7 — Gas Cylinders

### 3.7.1 Storage (6)
1. All cylinders stacked **vertically** (full or empty) and secured against falling
2. Full cylinders kept separate from empty ones
3. All cylinders shielded from direct sunlight or other heat (avoid excess internal pressure, leakage, or bursting)
4. Oxygen cylinders stored at least **6 metres** from acetylene or LPG cylinders (oxygen + fuel gas mixture from leakage = highly explosive)
5. Oxygen cylinders not kept in same storeroom as LPG or acetylene cylinders
6. Refer to **CoP 1.0 — Hazardous Substances** and **CoP 49.0 — Compressed Gases and Air**

### 3.7.2 Cylinder Handling (10)
1. Hands and clothing free from grit, grease and oil when handling cylinders
2. Nozzles not used for handling purposes (not designed for such weight/stress)
3. Cylinders in use normally kept and moved in **purpose built trolleys**
4. If moving cylinders not in a trolley: remove regulators and hoses; verify valves shut
5. Cylinders never rolled along the ground
6. If lifted by crane: secured in a **special carrier** only — **never** with chain or wire rope slings
7. Treated with care; not subjected to shocks or falls
8. When transported in vehicle: secured to prevent injury from sudden movement; not dropped when unloaded
9. Acetylene cylinders always transported and used in the **vertical position** — if accidentally horizontal, stood upright for **15 minutes** to settle before use
10. Refer to **CoP 1.0** and **CoP 49.0**

### 3.7.3 Cylinder Attachments (10)
1. Regulators always fitted to reduce cylinder pressure to working pressure of blowpipe
2. Only regulators designed for the specific gas and rated for full cylinder pressure
3. Cylinder valve "cracked open" before fitting regulator (clears dust and foreign matter)
4. Adjusting screw of regulator always released before opening cylinder valve; valve opened gradually
5. Periodic checks to ensure no gas leaking when pressure regulating screw at zero — **checks made with water containing detergent only** (bubbles indicate leak)
6. Hoses: kept for one type of gas only; colour coded (red = acetylene/fuel gases except LPG; orange = LPG; blue = oxygen); inspected **daily** before use for cuts/scratches/cracks/burnt or worn patches; effectively clipped or crimped to equipment and protected from sharp edges, falling metal, traffic, sparks
7. Gas mixtures prevented — non-return valves (flashback arrestors) fitted to each blowpipe inlet connection
8. High-risk flashback arrestor situations (mandatory): confined spaces with difficult access; operations under hot work permit adjacent to live plant; near compressed air workings; when employees under training; where device in gas line has significant internal volume (e.g. welding flux container)
9. Blowpipes dismantled and cleaned at regular intervals
10. Refer to **CoP 49.0 — Compressed Gases and Air**

### 3.7.4 Inspection Schedule
Employers shall ensure:
1. Visual checks by users on welding and cutting equipment **before use**
2. Formal inspections by competent persons at regular intervals per manufacturers' specification, at minimum:
   - **Daily:** inspection and leak test all joints at working pressure
   - **Weekly** (if in constant use) **or before every use**
   - **6-monthly:** functional test by a competent person
   - **5-yearly** (or per manufacturer recommendation): refurbishment or replacement — elastomers and seals deteriorate in and out of service; items stored **≥1 year without use** shall be inspected per annual maintenance inspection
3. Malfunctioning equipment immediately removed from service
